Litigation Visual Strategy · Biomedical Visualization · Legal Technology

I take complicated legal, business, scientific, and technical information and work out the clearest way to explain it.

The answer might be a trial demonstrative that helps develop a case theme, a visual explanation of a corporate structure or financial transaction, a presentation for firm leadership or a client, a three-dimensional model, or a better way to introduce new technology. I bring fifteen years inside litigation, including complex corporate, ERISA, and medical-malpractice matters, along with graduate training in visual art, visual theory, legal visualization, adult-learning theory, and instructional design.

I work with attorneys, clients, and other subject-matter experts to understand the underlying record, the audience, and what the presentation needs to accomplish. From there, I develop and manage the work from the initial idea through review and final delivery, with close attention to accuracy, clarity, and how the material will actually be used. My current research into three-dimensional anatomical models and juror comprehension is one part of that larger interest in how people understand complex evidence.

About

Litigation, visual practice, and learning design

For more than a decade I ran the daily operations of a boutique litigation firm and built its communications, presentations, diagrams, timelines, and brand materials because the work needed to be done. That pulled me toward graduate training in fine art and biomedical visualization, which is really the discipline of taking complex, technical material and making it usable for a specific audience. I now assist with trial strategy, evidence synthesis, and trial preparation and visualization with a tier-one medical-malpractice firm.

I bring the same instinct and training to everything I work on, whether the subject is a complicated corporate transaction, the relationships among parties in a case, a timeline drawn from thousands of pages of records, a deck that helps an attorney see the structure of an argument, a visual that helps a client understand a difficult trial strategy, a surgical procedure, a patient facing a decision, or a new technology a team has to learn.

Most recently I have been one of the people helping my firm adopt AI, work that is really applied instructional and communication design, and this fall I begin a part-time evening J.D. My decision to pursue a part-time J.D. is closely connected to this work. I want a deeper understanding of the rules of evidence, procedure, expert testimony, and the legal standards that shape how demonstratives are developed and used. An effective trial visual is not simply a well-designed image. It has to address the right issue, accurately reflect the record, support the larger case strategy, and anticipate how the evidence may be introduced or challenged. Learning the law more deeply will help me work with trial teams earlier in that process and become a better strategic partner to the attorneys and clients I support. A full account of my litigation and firm-operations experience is on my résumé.
