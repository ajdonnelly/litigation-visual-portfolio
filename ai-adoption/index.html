AI adoption · a body of work inside a litigation firm

This is AI work I have been doing inside a litigation firm.

Most recently I have been one of the people helping my firm adopt generative AI. That has meant testing tools, building guardrails, coordinating with stakeholders across the firm, and creating the training and materials that help colleagues actually take it up.

It turned out to be very close to what a litigation creative and visual-strategy team does. The through-line is the same: coordinate with the people who own the work, get hands-on with the technology, and build deliverables that help a specific audience take in complex information and learn from it. That is the overlap I would bring to this role.

The deliverables

A lot of hands-on production

This work meant building things, not just advising. To help people actually take up AI, I had to produce a range of deliverables across formats: a strategy deck, interactive training modules, a tracker, worksheets and canvases, and a short audio briefing, each aimed at a different audience and a different way of learning.

That is the part that maps directly to this role. A litigation creative and visual-strategy team lives on exactly this: turning complex material into clear, well-made deliverables in whatever format the audience needs, and doing the hands-on production to get there. The pieces below are that work, generalized and de-identified to protect client and firm confidentiality.

Selected work · The blueprint deck

The 90-Day Paralegal AI Blueprint

A pilot laid out from a paralegal’s seat: how to elevate everyday paralegal workflows with structured, responsible AI over ninety days. It covers the goal, non-negotiable principles, a human-in-the-loop review model, scope, a phased timeline, and how to measure it. Adapted from role-specific enablement work I created in practice, generalized and de-identified to protect client and firm confidentiality.
